What

Twelve stations are shouting.
One of them started it.

When a system breaks, everything downstream breaks too — and the parts nearest the customer scream loudest.

So the loudest alarm is almost never the cause. Finding the real one means reading a room's worth of data from one bad half-hour.

We built a machine that reads all of it in one second and says when, which, and why.

Why it matters

When our machine is sure, it's usually wrong.

It writes down how confident it is on every case. We checked whether that meant anything.

It's backwards. Three times more accurate when it doubts itself. We tested it — this isn't luck.

Everyone builds the same safety net: "if the machine isn't sure, ask a human." Here that hands people the easy ones and lets the mistakes through.

A clean picture means "nothing's wrong" —
or "the fire is somewhere I can't see."

From inside the thermometer, those look identical.

How

It measures. Then something else grades it.

For every dial: "is this unusual for this machine, tonight?" Rank them, date the earliest move, write a report.

Every number in that report is one it measured.
Nothing invented — a made-up detail sends someone chasing nothing at 3am.

Then a second AI reads the report and marks it. It gave us 3 out of 5 on "would this help someone at 3am."

We published the bad grade. We also tested the AI as the decision-maker — twice — and it changed 0 of 20 answers, so it doesn't get that job.
